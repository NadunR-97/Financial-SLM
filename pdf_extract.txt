Model Validation Approach   
Evaluation Methodology  
Both models use the same evaluation framework:  
• Metrics Calculated:  
 MAE (Mean Absolute Error)  
 RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error)  
 MAPE (Mean Absolute Percentage Error)  
  
• Evaluation Process:  
 Temporal train/test split (no random splitting)  
 Metrics calculated on held-out recent data  
 Identical visual comparison of actual vs predicted  
  
 
  
• Key Points on Model Evaluation Periods:

Prophet Model Evaluation: The historical performance metrics (MAE, RMSE, MAPE) for the 
Prophet model were calculated by training the model on the historical data excluding the last 
3 months and then evaluating its predictions against those last 3 months.  
Random Forest Model Evaluation: The historical performance metrics for the Random Forest 
Regressor were calculated by training the model on the historical data excluding the last 20% 
and then evaluating its predictions against that last 20% of the data.  
Implication: Because the test periods used for calculating these evaluation metrics are 
different (last 3 months vs. last 20% of data), the metrics for the two models are not directly 
comparable over the exact same historical time window.  
For the purpose of evaluating the historical performance of the predictive models, a train-test 
split approach was utilized. The Prophet model's evaluation metrics (MAE, RMSE, MAPE) 
were calculated based on forecasting the last 3 months of the historical data.  
In contrast, the Random Forest Regressor's evaluation metrics were derived from predicting 
the last 20% of its respective historical data. It is important to note that due to this difference 
in the test set time periods, the evaluation metrics provided for the two models are not 
directly comparable in a strict, like-for-like manner. Each model's metrics reflect its 
performance over a distinct portion of the historical timeline.

To assess the performance of the models in predicting Overall Total Sales, three common 
evaluation metrics were used: MAE, RMSE, and MAPE. These metrics help determine how 
close the predicted values are to the actual sales data.  
1. Mean Absolute Error (MAE)  
MAE measures the average absolute difference between predicted and actual values, without 
considering the direction of the error.  
On average, how much did the model's predictions differ from the actual sales figures (in 
dollar terms)?  
Interpretation: Lower MAE = Better accuracy.  
• Results:  
 Prophet: $1,362,710.68  
 Random Forest: $1,279,294.73  
 Conclusion: Random Forest had a lower MAE, meaning its predictions were closer to 
actual values by an average of $83,415.95 compared to Prophet.  
2. Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)  
RMSE is similar to MAE but squares the errors before averaging and then takes the square 
root. This means larger errors are penalized more heavily.  
In simple terms: It shows how significant the prediction errors were, with more weight given 
to large mistakes.  
Interpretation: Lower RMSE = Fewer large errors.

3. Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)  
MAPE expresses the average error as a percentage of the actual values.  
In simple terms: It shows how far off the predictions were, on average, in percentage terms.

CONCLUSION & FUTURE WORK  
  
In conclusion, managing the security challenges in big data within organizations is important 
for organizations to secure sensitive information, maintain regulatory compliance, and retain 
stakeholder trust. Unauthorized access, data breaches and data volume had the most 
significant impact on organizations according to the survey findings and interview. To 
effectively tackle these challenges, organizations must take a comprehensive strategy that 
includes strategies such as strong access controls, encryption, data governance, and incident 
response mechanisms.

Key solutions included advanced encryption techniques, access control mechanisms, training 
for employees, data masking techniques and utilizing extensive security frameworks. By 
utilizing the strategies and recommendation recommended, organizations can effectively 
mitigate the security challenges in big data and also establish a secure big data environment 
within the organization.
